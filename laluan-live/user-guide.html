Foooverse / LaluanLive Docs

User Guide

Welcome to LaluanLive — a community-powered way to check live status and report issues on rail lines, brought to you by Foooverse.

Getting Started

No sign-up is required. Open the app and you'll see the current status of all six lines: Kelana Jaya, Ampang, Sri Petaling, Kajang, Putrajaya and Shah Alam.

Checking Line Status

The main screen shows every line as a card with its code, its name and a status label with an icon:

  • Normal — no active problems reported
  • Delayed — a problem is slowing service
  • Disrupted — a more serious problem is affecting service
  • Halted — service has stopped

The header at the top says how many lines are affected (for example "2 of 6 lines affected"), when the information was last updated, and roughly how many people are using the app right now.

  • A line running normally is a single compact row with a short note, such as "No active reports" or "4 riders say it's running fine".
  • An affected line expands to show the reason, where the information came from (Official or Rider report) and when.
  • Tap the star on a line to pin it as your line. It's shown first from then on, and is remembered on your device only.
  • Latest updates, all lines lists the five newest items across the network.

Tap a line to open its page.

A Line's Page

Each line has its own page and its own link, and Back takes you to the board. From top to bottom:

  1. Status — the line's current status and the reason for it.
  2. Alternatives — options for getting around a problem (see "Finding Another Way" below).
  3. Official updates (Rapid KL) — notices from RapidKL, with the ones still active first. If there are none you'll see "No Rapid KL notice for this line right now", with a link to RapidKL's Pulse page.
  4. Rider reports (Unverified) — reports from other riders, each with Agree and Flag buttons.
  5. News and social — news coverage, folded away until you open it.
  6. Stations — folded away until you open it. The line is drawn as a rail, with interchange stations marked and stations that have reports highlighted. Tap a station to see only the updates for that station.

At the bottom of the page, Report a problem and Running fine sit side by side.

On a Computer

On a wide screen (about 1024 pixels and up) the app shows three panes: the lines on the left, the updates in the middle (with filter tabs: All, Official, Rider reports, News & social), and the details on the right.

Refreshing

Pull down on the screen to refresh. The app also refreshes by itself. What you see can be up to about 30 seconds behind, because the data is cached briefly to keep the app fast.

Reading an Update

Every update shows where it came from, plus a clock time and how long ago, for example "8:02 am · 12 min ago":

  • Official · Rapid KL — a notice from RapidKL's own service announcements.
  • Rider report · Unverified — a report from another rider. It's labelled Unverified because RapidKL hasn't confirmed it; other riders can Agree with it or Flag it.
  • News — coverage from a news outlet, with the publisher credited and a "Read article" link.

Official notices come from RapidKL's service announcements, which RapidKL publishes in Malay and English. LaluanLive shows the English version. They can lag behind what's happening on the platform, so they aren't a live feed. Some mention an expected repair or resume time. When RapidKL announces that service has been restored, that shows up as a resolved update and the earlier open items for that line are closed.

News is third-party reporting, not RapidKL's own words. It never affects a line's status and is removed 48 hours after it was published.

Older rider reports may include a photo. New reports don't, because photos are no longer collected.

How a Line's Status Is Decided

Rider reports from the last hour are combined to work out a line's status:

  • Each device counts once, using its most serious report.
  • More serious problem types count for more. A breakdown or a delay counts for more than crowding or an "other" report.
  • One breakdown or delay report is enough to mark a line Delayed. A single crowding report is not. Disrupted takes more, or more serious, reports, and Halted needs reports from several different riders.
  • RapidKL notices set a minimum: the line is at least as bad as the notice says. The final status is whichever is worse, the riders' picture or RapidKL's notice.
  • News, Agree and Running fine never change the status directly.

When an Issue Closes

Open items are checked every few minutes and close in these cases:

  • RapidKL says service is back. When RapidKL announces that service has been restored, the earlier open items for that line close.
  • Quiet for 30 minutes. If a line sees no new activity for 30 minutes, its open items close. Official notices with a repair target more than 6 hours after they were published are the exception. They stay open until RapidKL says it's over or replaces them.
  • Riders say it's running fine. When three different riders confirm a line is running fine, an open rider report on it can close early. This doesn't happen while a RapidKL notice for that line is still open.

A repair date never closes anything by itself. If it passes with no update, the notice is marked "date passed, awaiting update". Closed items are deleted 48 hours later.

Reporting a Problem

  1. Tap Report a problem at the bottom of the screen. From the board you pick the line first. From a line's page, the line is already filled in.
  2. Pick the problem: Breakdown, Delay, Crowding or Other.
  3. Optionally choose the station and add a short note.
  4. Tap Send.

A short confirmation appears, and your report shows up as "Rider report · Unverified". There's no photo to attach.

To keep the feed trustworthy there are limits: one report per line every 5 minutes, and up to 6 reports an hour. If you hit a limit, the app tells you.

Agreeing with a Report

If another rider's report matches what you're seeing, tap Agree to back it up. You can't agree with your own report or with one that's already resolved, and you can withdraw your agreement later. Agree helps other riders see how many people are noticing the same thing, but it doesn't change a line's status on its own.

Flagging a Report

If a report looks wrong, unrelated, spam, or contains sensitive content, tap Flag and choose the reason that fits best:

  • Sensitive or inappropriate
  • Wrong or unrelated photo
  • Not relevant to this line or station
  • Spam or fake report

Flags are final and can't be undone, so please only flag reports that genuinely need it. A report is removed once enough different riders have flagged it: 2 flags for sensitive content, 3 for any other reason.

Confirming a Line Is Running Fine

At the bottom of a line's page, tap Running fine to let other riders know things seem normal. It's one tap, with no note needed, and you can confirm each line once an hour.

Confirmations don't appear in the feed, and they can't be flagged. On the board, a normal line may show how many riders say it's running fine, for example "4 riders say it's running fine". As described above, when three riders confirm, an open rider report can close early. Confirmations never change a line's status directly.

Finding Another Way

A line's page has an alternatives section for when things go wrong. It can include:

  • Alternative service details, when RapidKL's notice mentions them, such as trains turning back at a station, where to change trains, or replacement buses. This is RapidKL's own information.
  • A link to open Google Maps at the affected station, so you can plan a route to wherever you're going.
  • A link to find a ride through a ride-hailing app (Grab).

LaluanLive doesn't know where you are or where you're headed, and it doesn't plan routes itself. Those links hand you over to other apps and services, which have their own terms and privacy policies.

People Using the App

The header shows roughly how many people are using LaluanLive right now, across the whole app. It's a rough indicator of activity, not an exact or verified count.

About Your Data

  • No account or personal information is required. Your device gets a random anonymous ID, kept in a cookie and local storage, which is used to apply the limits above.
  • Reports and the incidents they belong to are deleted 48 hours after they're resolved. News is deleted 48 hours after it was published.
  • A permanent per-line summary of past incidents (dates and counts only) is kept, so the app can show how often a line has problems.
  • The How this works page in the app explains where updates come from, what each status means, how reporting and Running fine work, the anti-spam rules, and what data is kept.

For full details, see our Privacy Policy.

If Reporting Is Disabled on Your Device

If 5 of your reports have been removed by other riders' flags, this device can no longer report or confirm that a line is running fine. You can still see everything else as normal. If you think this happened by mistake, email support@foooverse.com.

Light and Dark Mode

The app matches your device's light or dark setting when you first open it. Use the theme toggle to switch manually at any time. Once you do, your choice is remembered and your device's setting won't override it again.
