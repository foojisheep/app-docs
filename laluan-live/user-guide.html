User Guide

Welcome to LaluanLive — a community-powered way to check live status and report issues on rail lines, brought to you by Foooverse.

Getting Started

No sign-up is required. Open the app and you'll see the current status of all six lines: Kelana Jaya, Ampang, Sri Petaling, Kajang, Putrajaya and Shah Alam.

Tip: add LaluanLive to your phone's Home Screen so it opens like an app. On iPhone this is also what lets you get alerts (see "Getting Alerts for Your Lines" below).

Checking Line Status

The main screen shows every line as a card with its code, its name and a status label with an icon:

  • Normal — no active problems reported
  • Delayed — a problem is slowing service
  • Disrupted — a more serious problem is affecting service
  • Halted — service has stopped

The header at the top says how many lines are affected (for example "2 of 6 lines affected"), when the information was last updated, and roughly how many people are using the app right now.

  • A line running normally is a single compact row with a short note, such as "No active reports" or "4 riders say it's running fine".
  • An affected line expands to show the reason, where the information came from (Official or Rider report) and when.
  • When a line's status changes while you have the app open, its card is outlined once in the new status colour and says Just changed for about a minute and a half, so you can spot it at a glance.
  • Tap the star on a line to pin it as your line. It's shown first from then on, and is remembered on your device only. If your line changes while the app is open, you also get a short message at the top of the screen, and Android phones vibrate.
  • Latest updates, all lines lists the five newest items across the network.

Tap a line to open its page.

A Line's Page

Each line has its own page and its own link, and Back takes you to the board. At the top, next to the line's name, are the star (pin as your line) and the bell (alerts for this line). From top to bottom:

  1. Status — the line's current status and the reason for it.
  2. Need another way? — only while the line is affected: feeder buses and links for getting around the problem (see "Finding Another Way" below).
  3. Official updates (Rapid KL) — notices from RapidKL, with the ones still active first. If there are none you'll see "No Rapid KL notice for this line right now", with a link to RapidKL's Pulse page.
  4. Rider reports (Unverified) — reports from other riders, each with an Agree button.
  5. Stations — folded away until you open it. The line is drawn as a rail, with interchange stations marked and stations that have reports highlighted. Tap a station to see only the updates for that station.

At the bottom of the page, Report a problem and Running fine sit side by side.

On a Computer

On a wide screen (about 1024 pixels and up) the app shows three panes: the lines on the left, the updates in the middle (with filter tabs: All, Official, Rider reports), and the details on the right.

Refreshing

Pull down on the screen to refresh. While the app is open it also refreshes by itself every minute. What you see is usually less than a minute old and at most a couple of minutes, because the data is cached briefly to keep the app fast.

Getting Alerts for Your Lines

Tap the bell on a line's page to get a notification when that line becomes delayed, disrupted or halted, and again when it's back to normal. You can follow as many lines as you like, for example the two lines you take to work. Alerts arrive even when LaluanLive is closed.

On iPhone or iPad

iPhone only sends alerts from websites added to the Home Screen (iOS 16.4 or later). If you tap the bell in Safari, the app shows you these steps:

  1. In Safari, tap Share.
  2. Choose Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  3. Open LaluanLive from your Home Screen, go to your line and tap the bell.
  4. When asked, tap Allow to let LaluanLive send notifications.

On Android or a computer

Tap the bell and allow notifications when your browser asks. That's it.

What an alert looks like

  • "Kelana Jaya Line: delayed — Official notice from Rapid KL." when RapidKL has posted a notice.
  • "Kelana Jaya Line: disrupted — Reported by several riders (not yet confirmed by Rapid KL)." when the change comes from rider reports.
  • "Kelana Jaya Line back to normal" when the problem is over.

Tap an alert to open that line. A newer alert for the same line replaces the older one, so your notifications don't pile up.

Alerts can arrive up to about 5 minutes after a line's status changes. They follow your phone's own notification, sound and Focus settings. An alert that couldn't be delivered within 30 minutes (for example because your phone was off) is dropped rather than shown late.

Turning alerts off

Tap the bell again on that line. To stop all alerts, turn off the bell on every line you follow, or turn off notifications for LaluanLive in your phone's settings. If you blocked notifications earlier and want them back, allow them in your phone's settings, then tap the bell again.

Reading an Update

Every update shows where it came from, plus a clock time and how long ago, for example "8:02 am · 12 min ago":

  • Official · Rapid KL — a notice from RapidKL's own service announcements.
  • Rider report · Unverified — a report from another rider, such as "Delay reported" with the station. It's labelled Unverified because RapidKL hasn't confirmed it; other riders can Agree with it.

Official notices come from the service announcements RapidKL publishes on its website, which LaluanLive checks automatically every 10 minutes at peak times (5–10 am and 4–9 pm), every 30 minutes at other times and every 2 hours overnight. Each one is shown with a short English title, such as "Train delay: Kelana Jaya Line · Gombak" or "Service restored: Putrajaya Line". A notice that covers more than one line (for example the Ampang and Sri Petaling Lines) shows on each of those lines, and when it names a station, that station is marked on the line's page. Tap View official notice to read RapidKL's full announcement, which is in Malay and English. Notices can lag behind what's happening on the platform, so they aren't a live feed. When RapidKL announces that service has been restored, that shows up as a resolved update and the earlier open items for that line are closed. RapidKL's general updates are shown too, titled "Update", for example "Update: Kajang Line · Maluri". Because LaluanLive only sees each update's title, it can't tell whether it's about a problem, so updates never change a line's status or send alerts; tap View official notice to read them. Train frequency schedules and bus notices aren't shown.

If LaluanLive can't reach RapidKL's announcements for a while, a banner at the top says official notices may be delayed, so a quiet board never pretends everything is fine.

Older rider reports may include a photo or a short note. New reports don't, because only the problem and station are collected now.

How a Line's Status Is Decided

Rider reports from the last hour are combined to work out a line's status:

  • Each device counts once, using its most serious report.
  • More serious problem types count for more. A breakdown or a delay counts for more than crowding or an "other" report.
  • One breakdown or delay report is enough to mark a line Delayed. A single crowding report is not. Disrupted takes more, or more serious, reports, and Halted needs reports from several different riders.
  • RapidKL notices set a minimum: the line is at least as bad as the notice says. The final status is whichever is worse, the riders' picture or RapidKL's notice.
  • Agree and Running fine never change the status directly.

When an Issue Closes

Open items are checked every few minutes and close in these cases:

  • RapidKL says service is back. When RapidKL announces that service has been restored, the earlier open items for that line close.
  • Quiet for 30 minutes. If a line sees no new activity for 30 minutes, its open items close. Official notices with a repair target more than 6 hours after they were published are the exception. They stay open until RapidKL says it's over or replaces them.
  • Riders say it's running fine. When three different riders confirm a line is running fine, an open rider report on it can close early. This doesn't happen while a RapidKL notice for that line is still open.

A repair date never closes anything by itself. If it passes with no update, the notice is marked "date passed, awaiting update". Closed items are deleted 48 hours later.

Reporting a Problem

  1. Tap Report a problem at the bottom of the screen. From the board you pick the line first. From a line's page, the line is already filled in.
  2. Pick the problem: Breakdown, Delay, Crowding or Other.
  3. Optionally choose the station.
  4. Tap Send.

A short confirmation appears, and your report shows up as "Rider report · Unverified", for example "Delay reported" at the station you chose. There's no photo or note to add.

To keep the feed fair there are limits: one report per line every 5 minutes, and up to 6 reports an hour. If you hit a limit, the app tells you.

Your report closes by itself once the line has been quiet for 30 minutes, or sooner when three different riders say the line is running fine. Nobody can flag or remove it, and reporting is never blocked for your device.

Agreeing with a Report

If another rider's report matches what you're seeing, tap Agree to back it up. You can't agree with your own report or with one that's already resolved, and you can withdraw your agreement later. Agree helps other riders see how many people are noticing the same thing, but it doesn't change a line's status on its own.

Confirming a Line Is Running Fine

At the bottom of a line's page, tap Running fine to let other riders know things seem normal. It's one tap, with no note needed, and you can confirm each line once an hour.

Confirmations don't appear in the feed. On the board, a normal line may show how many riders say it's running fine, for example "4 riders say it's running fine". As described above, when three riders confirm, an open rider report can close early. Confirmations never change a line's status directly.

Finding Another Way

When a line is delayed, disrupted or halted, its page shows a Need another way? section. It's hidden while the line is running normally. It can include:

  • Feeder buses nearby: live positions of MRT feeder buses serving the affected station, refreshed every 30 seconds, when that station has feeder routes.
  • Alternative service details from RapidKL, such as trains turning back at a station or replacement buses, when they're available. Most RapidKL notices only reach LaluanLive as a short title, so this is often missing. Tap View official notice for RapidKL's full details.
  • Open in Google Maps, which opens Google Maps at the affected station so you can plan a route to wherever you're going.
  • Search a ride, which opens Grab's website so you can book a ride. It doesn't fill in your pickup point.

The "affected station" is the station you've tapped on the line's rail, or otherwise the station named in the latest report or notice. LaluanLive doesn't know where you are or where you're headed, and it doesn't plan routes itself. These links hand you over to other apps and services, which have their own terms and privacy policies.

People Using the App

The header shows roughly how many people are using LaluanLive right now, across the whole app. It's a rough indicator of activity, not an exact or verified count.

About Your Data

  • No account or personal information is required. Your device gets a random anonymous ID, kept in a cookie and local storage, which is used to apply the limits above.
  • Reports and the incidents they belong to are deleted 48 hours after they're resolved.
  • A permanent per-line summary of past incidents (dates and counts only) is kept, so the app can show how often a line has problems.
  • If you turn on alerts, LaluanLive keeps your browser's push address (needed to deliver notifications) and the list of lines you follow. It isn't linked to your device ID or your reports. Turning off the bell on every line deletes it.
  • The How this works page in the app explains where updates come from, what each status means, how reporting, alerts and Running fine work, the reporting limits, and what data is kept.

For full details, see our Privacy Policy.

Light and Dark Mode

The app matches your device's light or dark setting when you first open it. Use the theme toggle at the top of the main screen (the one listing all lines) to switch manually at any time. Once you do, your choice is remembered and your device's setting won't override it again.
